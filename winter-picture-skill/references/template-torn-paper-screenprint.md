# Vintage Torn-Paper Screen-Print Template

Use this template for a vertical 9:16 archival editorial collage made from a faithful real photograph and aged handmade paper. Adapt source-specific subjects and colors without changing the structural rules.

## Required composition

- Canvas: vertical 9:16.
- Split the work into two materially distinct zones.
- Lower zone: faithful real photography from the uploaded image. Preserve authentic subjects, layout, light, color relationships, and photographic character. A restrained low-saturation vintage-film grade and fine photographic grain are allowed; never repaint or regenerate the photo.
- Upper zone: aged warm beige handmade fiber paper occupying 50%–65% of the canvas; default to about 58%.
- Join: use a genuinely irregular torn-paper boundary with fuzzy deckled fibers, rough fraying, loose paper debris, and uneven bites. Never use a smooth, straight, or clean cut.
- Tear opening: keep the most visible notch off-center, biased toward one side. Let the photograph rise through that asymmetric opening rather than centering the tear.

## Paper and print language

- Preserve subtle paper wrinkles, old-paper speckles, fine fibers, and gentle wear.
- Derive the screen-print motif only from important visible facts in the photo.
- Use sparse black or near-black distressed linework plus no more than two small muted color roles, typically a faded red/orange accent and a dusty blue block or line when supported by the photo.
- Prefer a few broken contours, axes, silhouettes, or masses over a complete scenic illustration.
- Make screen-print ink visibly analog: coarse grain, halftone noise, dry-brush gaps, worn edges, broken strokes, slight misregistration, and incomplete coverage.
- Keep generous negative space. The paper zone must not become a full illustration.

## Source adaptation block

Before the fixed style block, describe:

1. The uploaded image as the sole photographic source and the lower-zone edit target.
2. The photographic facts that must remain unchanged.
3. Three to five source-derived motifs to translate into screen-print marks.
4. Which one or two muted accent colors come from the source.
5. Whether the tear opening is biased left or right based on the source composition.

## Typography

- Use one short English sentence grounded in the visible scene.
- Render it exactly once, extremely small, in a faded low-contrast vintage typewriter face.
- Place it in the upper-right paper area by default. Use the lower-left paper area only when the screen-print motif or tear makes the upper-right unsuitable.
- Keep generous margins. Do not add a title, subtitle, date, number, or any other text.

## Prompt assembly order

1. Image role and photo-preservation statement.
2. 9:16 two-zone composition and exact paper/photo proportions.
3. Off-center torn boundary direction and material detail.
4. Source-derived distressed screen-print motif and limited palette.
5. Exact typewriter sentence and placement.
6. Overall quiet archival documentary mood.
7. Avoid list.

## Avoid list

No full-canvas illustration, no full-canvas photograph, no centered tear opening, no smooth or straight dividing edge, no clean vector linework, no literal complete scenic illustration, no oversized type, no 3D render, no anime, no cartoon, no thick impasto, no neon or fluorescent colors, no watermark, no outer border, no frame, no collage shadow, no mockup presentation, no close-up facial emphasis, and no extra decoration or text.

## Validation checklist

- The output is vertical 9:16.
- Aged paper occupies 50%–65%; real photography occupies the lower zone and remains photographic.
- The torn boundary is fibrous, rough, asymmetrical, and visibly off-center.
- Paper wrinkles, fibers, stains, and wear are subtle but visible.
- Screen-print marks are sparse, source-derived, grainy, worn, and non-vector.
- The typewriter sentence is tiny, low contrast, correct, and appears exactly once.
- Mood is quiet, expansive, low-saturation, and documentary rather than decorative or cartoonish.
